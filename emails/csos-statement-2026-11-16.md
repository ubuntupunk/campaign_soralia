Re: HOA notice to members regarding CSOS Adjudication Order, Case 518005 (2 November 2026)

1. Our position

Soralia Village Community Action is the residents' campaign behind Stop Automatic Handovers. Our position is set out at https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/ and remains unchanged:

automatic handovers of arrears accounts for collection are ultra vires clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 of the Soralia Village Constitution. Clause 8.6 permits the committee to hand over a debt outstanding for more than 30 days only as a discretionary committee decision on an individual account. Clause 7.6.8 requires committee approval for routine recovery action. A blanket handover by calendar date under the 15 July 2026 Hand Over Resolution removes that per-case approval and is therefore unauthorised by our governing document.

2. What the order actually decided

On 2 November 2026 the Community Schemes Ombud Service (CSOS) issued an adjudication order in Case 518005. The application was partly granted: the HOA was directed to reverse receipting fees and arrears-account review fees of R955.94 in total, together with related interest, on that owner's levy account. The adjudicator found these charges were not substantiated under the applicable debt collectors' tariff, and that resolutions adopted on 15 July 2026 could not retrospectively authorise earlier charges.

The rest of the relief sought was dismissed. That included the prohibition we sought on the **unlawful practices** — automatic handovers which are ultra vires our Constitution, and the opportunistic receipting fees which are ultra vires the Debt Collectors Act. No order as to costs was made.

We note two things about the HOA's characterisation in its member notice of 16 November 2026.

First, the dismissal of that relief does not validate the practices challenged, and it is not a finding that collection action may proceed unchecked. What we sought was a prohibition on the unlawful circumvention of due process and on action contrary to our Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act — not a prohibition on legitimate debt collection. The adjudicator declined to rule on the Managing Agent's practices across other schemes because that falls outside what CSOS can grant in this proceeding. The order does not decide whether automatic handovers are lawful in general, and it does not decide whether receipting fees or arrears-review charges are lawful on any account other than the one before it.

Second, the order binds only the account in Complaint One — Mr David Lewis's account. It is persuasive, not binding, for every other owner. Each account stands on its own facts.

3. We welcome the review — but reserve our rights

We welcome the Board's undertaking, stated in its notice, to review its arrears and collection process with the Managing Agent "to make sure it is applied consistently with the Constitution." That is the right direction, and we will engage constructively.

We nevertheless reserve all our rights regarding the opportunistic collections procedures that remain in place, including:

- receipting fees charged per payment occasion;
- arrears-account review charges;
- interest levied on those charges;
- any account treated as handed over for collection without an individual committee decision.

Nothing in the CSOS order, and nothing in the HOA's notice, extinguishes those rights. The order reverses charges on one account only; it does not refund anyone else, and it does not prohibit the Board from continuing the same practices on other accounts.

4. Our position on debt collection and levies

We support legitimate debt collection practices that are aimed at restoring our village finances. We continue to urge greater action on the Municipal Debt, and we urge all homeowners and members to pay their levies on time. By the same token, we support the rights of our members to be free from unnecessary debt, and express our unwavering support for the most vulnerable amongst us, including seniors, veterans and disabled persons.

5. Call for an investigation across the scheme

We call on the Board of Trustees and the Managing Agent to investigate receipting fees, arrears-review charges, and automatic handovers across the entire scheme — not only on the account that was the subject of Case 518005. Members should check their own statements for similar charges and query anything unclear in writing to Tanya van Zyl at tanyav@trafalgar.co.za — the address the Board published in its 16 November 2026 notice for board and managing-agent queries.

If the Board's review concludes that these charges were imposed without constitutional or statutory authority, we expect the same relief to be extended to every affected account, not only to the one account the CSOS order reached.

6. Not legal advice

This statement is issued by Soralia Village Community Action on behalf of its members and supporters, and does not constitute legal advice. Individual members whether charges on their own account should obtain their own attorney's review.
