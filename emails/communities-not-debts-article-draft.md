# Communities, Not Debt Collection Schemes

_Published at `/communities-not-debts.html` on 27 Nov 2026. This file is retained as the source draft; the editorial notes below record the corrections made before publication. The principal confirmed on 27 Nov 2026 that attorney review is complete._

A homeowners' association is a community of people and households. It is not a debt collection scheme with gardens and security gates attached. That distinction is not sentiment; it is the legal and constitutional character of the body we live in. Soralia Village is a common-law homeowners' association, not a body corporate. We are organised around a certified Constitution, and that Constitution is itself dependent on the laws of the Republic of South Africa and the City of Cape Town's bylaws. When a managing agent treats the levy roll primarily as a revenue pipeline, and when trustees allow that to happen, the association stops behaving like the community its members agreed to join.

This article sets out why democratic governance matters most to the people least able to absorb an abrupt collection letter, what the financial record actually shows, where the Constitution was bypassed, and what we propose to do about the municipal debt and the arrears book. We support legitimate debt collection aimed at restoring the village's finances, and we urge members to pay their levies on time. What we oppose is the unlawful circumvention of due process and action contrary to the Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act.

## 1. A community under a Constitution, not an administrative convenience

Our Constitution is the agreement members have made with one another about how decisions are taken, who may stand for election, how votes are counted and who may act for the association. It binds the association, its committee and everyone acting on its behalf. In Case 518015 the adjudicator put it plainly:

> "A homeowners association is governed by its constitution, which binds the association and its members as a contract. The association, its committee and those who act on its behalf, including a managing agent who chairs a general meeting, are equally bound to act in accordance with those instruments. A voluntary body that departs from its own constitution in taking a decision acts outside its powers." ([Case 518015 order, para 6.5](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-adjudication-order-518015.pdf))

That is why procedure is not a technicality. Clause 5.6 says: "At all meetings votes on any matter shall be by show of hands of those present and the majority of the votes in favour of a matter shall be binding." The adjudicator found that no show of hands was recorded on any item at the 13 July 2026 AGM, that the resolutions were voted on electronically and by paper ballot instead, and that on the association's own version the paper ballots of members physically present were captured two days after the meeting. The results were declared before the votes of everyone present had been counted. General meetings, the order says, are where members exercise their democratic rights, and "the integrity of the vote is foundational to the proper governance of the scheme" (paras 6.7–6.10).

Democratic governance matters everywhere, but it matters most to the households with the least margin for error. Our community includes seniors, people with disabilities and military veterans living on fixed incomes. A pension that arrives late, a delayed grant or a medical bill in the same month as the levy can turn an account into a crisis. When a procedure designed to protect each account is replaced by a calendar rule applied to everybody, it is these households that feel the collection fees, the demand letters and the credit pressure first. A healthy community does not squeeze its most vulnerable members while leaving the larger municipal problem untouched.

## 2. What the books showed at the change of managing agent

The transition from Residentia Trust to Trafalgar Property Management was recorded in the September 2025 special general meeting minutes: Residentia's management agreement ended on 31 December 2025, and members questioned the cost-effectiveness of the existing fee of R23,286 per month (which included CSOS submissions, payroll administration and the WeConnectU licence, but excluded stationery and arrears notifications). Trafalgar was appointed as the new managing agent; the 2025 annual financial statements record a management fee of R265,599 for that year.

We should be exact about the audit, because the record is often misquoted. The 2025 annual financial statements received an **unmodified** audit opinion. What the auditors did say, under "Emphasis of matter", was that they were "unable to comment on whether funds are sufficient for the up-keep, control, management and administration of the homeowner's association", and under "Recoverability of long outstanding debtors" that they "were unable to establish the recoverability of the levies in arrears amounting to R454,557 as shown per note 2". The Compliance Portfolio Review in the July 2026 AGM pack identified "limited financial accountability measures" and recommended an "annual independent audit or financial review". That is serious enough without calling it a qualified audit.

The liabilities behind those words are substantial. Utilities cost R1,999,688 in 2025 — R1,043,113 for water, R828,139 for sewerage and R128,436 for electricity — against an operating deficit of R285,636. Trade and other receivables stood at R465,474, of which R454,557 was levies in arrears. This is the sizeable debtors book that was handed to the new managing agent, alongside a municipal bill that is by far the largest single item in the budget.

The 2026 budget itself tells us how weak the recovery position is: the water and sewerage lines are explicitly marked "Provision for 45% recovery rate". That is a budgeting assumption about how much of what we bill we expect to collect — not a statistic about residents. It means the shortfall is carried by the scheme as a whole and, in the end, by every household through the levy. The supporting submission in Case 518015 notes that a 43.69% increase would have been needed to break even on that basis, against the modest increase adopted at the AGM. We cannot fix that hole by writing more collection fees to the people least able to pay.

## 3. The handover that cast the net too wide

Two provisions of our Constitution govern recovery. Clause 8.6 provides: "The committee may handover any debt due to the committee to the committee's attorneys, or debt collectors, for collection if the debt is outstanding for more than 30 (Thirty) days." Clause 7.6.8 allows routine legal action to recover lawfully imposed amounts, but only by way of an exception "after approval of the Homeowner's Association committee". Both vest a decision in the committee. Neither says that every account over a threshold is automatically handed over by operation of a calendar date.

Yet the practice complained about was that an account _not settled in full by the seventh day of the month_ was automatically considered handed over for debt collection, and a blanket handover resolution of 15 July 2026 gave Trafalgar broad discretion to act. The result was a public and embarrassing dispute before the Community Schemes Ombud Service in Case 518005. The adjudicator found that the July resolution was adopted after the May and June receipting fees had been charged and after the July charge was raised, and that the association had not shown how it retrospectively authorised those charges. On the decisive question the order says:

> "The evidence is sufficient to conclude that the Respondent has not adequately demonstrated that the disputed fees were imposed in accordance with the handover requirements it relies upon." ([Case 518005 order, para 190](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-adjudication-order-518005.pdf))

On receipting fees specifically, we are blunt. A charge for the act of receiving a levy payment is irrational: it penalises the member for paying. Applied by a blanket calendar rule rather than by a decision on the account, it is opportunistic, and it swells the very balance later used to justify handover or the suspension of voting rights. In our [submission in Case 518005](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/CSOS_518005_Final_Submission.pdf) we argued that the Debt Collectors Act does not support such a charge: section 8 requires a written mandate from the creditor for a specific debt before an agent may act as a debt collector and charge fees under the Act. We oppose receipting fees as irrational, opportunistic and unsupported by the Debt Collectors Act. The adjudicator did not decide the general lawfulness of receipting fees under the Act — he reversed the specified fees on the account before him and left the wider question open (paras 177–191, 202–203). This is our position, not a finding attributed to the order; and it is not opposition to legitimate collection, which we support, along with prompt payment of levies.

The order reversed specified receipting fees and arrears-review charges on the account before it and required interest to be recalculated. It expressly declined to prohibit automatic handovers across all schemes, and it declined a general moratorium on collection activity (paras 191, 202–203 and order 207). We say the same thing in our [statement on Case 518005](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-statement-518005.html): the order binds that account, and each account stands on its own facts. A handover must be a minuted committee decision on that account, under clauses 8.6 and 7.6.8, after written notice, a full breakdown and a genuine opportunity to pay, agree a plan or explain hardship. That is case-by-case treatment, and it is what the Constitution requires.

## 4. When governance passed out of the meeting

The second failure was more serious. In Case 518015 the order records that Trafalgar "is the managing agent of the HOA. Its representative chaired the annual general meeting in issue, and it prepared and delivered the HOA's response to the application. Trafalgar is not cited as a party" (para 2.3). Our Constitution reaffirms that general meetings are presided over by the elected Chairperson, or by a homeowner elected by the meeting, under clause 5.5. We have tabled a [member motion](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/draft-resolution-clause-5-5.docx) to reaffirm that presiding authority and to confine the managing agent to administrative support. It has been proposed and seconded by members and is ready to be placed on the agenda of the fresh general meeting; it is not an adopted resolution.

What happened at the AGM was not merely informal. The adjudicator declared that the electronic voting was not authorised by clause 5.6 and that all resolutions passed by electronic ballot were procedurally defective and of no force pending confirmation; directed a fresh general meeting within 30 days of delivery of the order, on at least 14 days' written notice, conducted by show of hands, to reconsider every 13 July resolution including the number of trustees and the committee election; declared the automatic suspension of the applicant's voting rights unlawful and held that no member's voting rights may be suspended under clause 5.9 on a balance including charges under dispute before the CSOS; declared clauses 6.1 and 6.2 the sole eligibility criteria for committee membership; and held that the applicant's nomination must be considered at the fresh meeting (paras 7.1–7.5). See the [order](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-adjudication-order-518015.pdf) and our [statement on Case 518015](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-statement-518015.html).

The trustees declared elected on 13 July remain in office until that meeting, and their interim acts are not invalidated by the order (para 7.11.1). Nobody says the HOA is dissolved. But the mandate given at the 13 July AGM expired when its outcome was nullified: the interim committee is a caretaker with standing only until members meet again. The presiding officer must consider every nomination without discrimination and only under clauses 6.1 and 6.2. An election conducted under those conditions is the beginning of democratic renewal, not the end of it.

## 5. What we will do next

**Take the municipal problem to the City.** The scale of the water and effluent debt and the 45% recovery assumption cannot be solved by collection fees on individual households. We will press for formal engagement with the City of Cape Town on relief that fits both the demographics and the financial footing of the village: individual household metering, a manageable payment arrangement for municipal arrears, technical assistance on bulk-meter reconciliation and non-potable use, and any indigency or hardship relief for which qualifying households may be eligible. Any approach must be properly authorised, and the figures must be tested against the current accounts before a proposal is tabled.

**Put the arrears book back under the Constitution.** Replace blanket handovers with a written, minuted process under clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6: notice, a full breakdown, a real chance to pay or agree a plan, and a recorded trustee decision on each account before any collection step. Hardship should be assessed case by case, with priority for seniors, people with disabilities and veterans. Legitimate collection continues; unlawful shortcuts stop.

**Finish the democratic reset.** Hold the fresh general meeting by show of hands with proper notice, reconsider all the 13 July resolutions, and let every eligible nomination be considered on the criteria in clauses 6.1 and 6.2. Then begin the constitutional refresh under clause 12, with the City's approval where the bylaws require it: clear standards for architectural decisions, defined financial thresholds, conflict-of-interest rules for dual roles, and an annual independent audit or financial review. Our draft [Community Charter](https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/charter.html) is a starting point for that conversation, not a binding rule until it is adopted and incorporated properly.

**Restore the separation of roles.** The Board decides policy and oversees finances; an onsite estate manager, if members choose to fund one, handles operations; the managing agent provides administration within the mandate members and trustees give it. A body that both administers the levy roll and earns fees from collecting it must never be left to write its own authority.

We will do the utmost to move the community forward onto a better footing. That means a village in which accounts are dealt with on their merits, where the vulnerable are protected by procedure rather than exposed by it, and where the big debt is tackled honestly instead of being passed down to the households least able to carry it. Read the draft resolution, attend the fresh meeting, pay your levy where you can, and hold us to these commitments.

## Editorial notes for review (remove before publication)

1. **"Qualified audit" — not supported, corrected.** The 2025 financial statements received an unmodified opinion (Theron du Plessis Durbanville Inc., per the audit report in the 13 July 2026 AGM pack). The auditors included an Emphasis of Matter and stated they were unable to establish recoverability of R454,557 in levies in arrears. The article says this instead. Source verified locally and against the Soralia HOA NotebookLM notebook, 27 Nov 2026.

2. **"Over 45% of the community" — not supported, corrected.** The only verified 45% in the record is "Provision for 45% recovery rate" against the 2026 water and sewerage budget lines in the AGM pack (also quoted in DRL17, _Soralia Water & Utilities Crisis_, CSOS 518015 supporting documents). It is a collection-rate assumption, not a demographic share. The article describes the vulnerable groups qualitatively, from the campaign's [BackaBuddy story](https://backabuddy.co.za/campaign/stop-soralia-village-automatic-handovers) and the _StratProposal_ in the scheme's records: seniors, people with disabilities and military veterans on social grants and pensions. If you hold a survey showing a demographic percentage, send the source and I will add it with attribution.

3. **"Our Chair handed governance over to the administrators" — narrowed.** The verified facts are that Trafalgar's representative chaired the AGM and prepared and delivered the HOA's response (518015, para 2.3), and that the adjudicator held agents chairing meetings are bound by the Constitution (para 6.5). There is no document in our records establishing a formal, deliberate handover of governance by the Chair. The article states the verified facts; if you have the source for the stronger claim, add it before publication.

4. **"Administrators were found to have circumvented our own rules" — narrowed.** Case 518005 found the association had not demonstrated that the disputed fees were imposed in accordance with its own handover requirements; it did not grant the blanket relief sought, and Trafalgar was not a party to Case 518015. The clear finding of departure from the Constitution is in 518015: electronic voting instead of the clause 5.6 show of hands, with those resolutions declared procedurally defective. The article keeps the two cases distinct. The new receipting-fees paragraph states **our** view that the charges are irrational, opportunistic and unsupported by the Debt Collectors Act (section 8 written mandate); the adjudicator did not make a general ruling on their lawfulness under the Act, and the article says so. Attorney review of the section 8 formulation was confirmed by the principal on 27 Nov 2026.

5. **"Case by case" is our reading, not a literal phrase in the Constitution.** Clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 vest the decision in the committee; they do not use the words "case by case". The article says "case-by-case treatment ... what the Constitution requires" as our interpretation. Keep it that way unless an attorney confirms the stronger claim.

6. **Motion status.** The principal confirmed on 27 Nov 2026 that the clause 5.5 motion now has a proposer and a seconder, and a signed scan exists locally (`Resolution - Restablishment of Democratic Chair.pdf`, repo root, untracked). The article says the motion is proposed and seconded and ready for the agenda, but not adopted. Member identifiers are deliberately **not** recorded in this tracked draft; keep the signed scan out of git. If you later need a typed copy with the details filled in, put it outside the repositories.

7. **Trafalgar framing trap.** Do not describe our position as opposing debt collection as such. Keep the standing distinction in paragraph 2 and section 5: we support legitimate collection and timely levy payment; we oppose unlawful circumvention of due process and action contrary to the Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act.
