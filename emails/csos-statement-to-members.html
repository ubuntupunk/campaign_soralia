Soralia Village Community Action statement on the CSOS Adjudication Order (Case 518005)
Soralia VC

Dear member,

Soralia Village Community Action has published a statement on the CSOS adjudication order in Case 518005, issued on 2 November 2026.

You can read the full statement here: https://soraliavillagecampaign.org.za/csos-statement.html

In brief:

Our position. Automatic handovers of arrears accounts for collection are ultra vires clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 of the Soralia Village Constitution. Clause 8.6 permits the committee to hand over a debt outstanding for more than 30 days only as a discretionary committee decision on an individual account. Clause 7.6.8 requires committee approval for routine recovery action. A blanket handover by calendar date under the 15 July 2026 Hand Over Resolution removes that per-case approval and is therefore unauthorised.

What the order decided. The CSOS order was partly granted: the HOA was directed to reverse receipting fees and arrears-account review fees of R955.94 in total, plus related interest, on one owner's account. The rest of the relief sought was dismissed — including the prohibition we sought on the unlawful practices: automatic handovers which are ultra vires our Constitution, and the opportunistic receipting fees which are ultra vires the Debt Collectors Act. No order as to costs was made.

What the order does not do. It binds only the account in Complaint One (David Lewis's account). It is persuasive, not binding, for every other owner. It does not decide whether automatic handovers are lawful in general, and it does not validate the unlawful practices that remain in place. What we oppose is the unlawful circumvention of due process and action contrary to our Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act — not legitimate debt collection.

Our position on debt collection and levies. We support legitimate debt collection practices aimed at restoring our village finances. We continue to urge greater action on the Municipal Debt, and we urge all homeowners and members to pay their levies on time. At the same time, we support the rights of our members to be free from unnecessary debt, and we support the most vulnerable amongst us, including seniors, veterans and disabled persons.

Call for investigation. We call on the Board of Trustees and the Managing Agent to investigate receipting fees, arrears-review charges, and automatic handovers across the entire scheme — not only on the account that was the subject of Case 518005. If the Board's review concludes these charges were imposed without constitutional or statutory authority, we expect the same relief to be extended to every affected account.

Members should check their own statements for similar charges and query anything unclear in writing to Tanya van Zyl at tanyav@trafalgar.co.za — the address the Board published in its 16 November 2026 notice for board and managing-agent queries.

This statement is not legal advice, and each account stands on its own facts.
