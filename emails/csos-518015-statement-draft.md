# Soralia Village Community Action — Statement on CSOS Case 518015

**Date:** 26 November 2026  
**Re:** The adjudication order of 13 November 2026, the interim committee and the fresh general meeting

The Community Schemes Ombud Service has ordered a fresh general meeting for Soralia Village. We set out below what that order means, what the interim committee may and may not do, and what members should expect when the meeting is called.

## 1. What the order does — and what it does not

Prayers 1 to 5 of the complaint were granted; prayers 6 to 9 were refused; there is no order as to costs. The adjudicator declared that the electronic voting system used at the annual general meeting of 13 July 2026 was not authorised by clause 5.6 of our Constitution, which prescribes voting by show of hands, and that all resolutions passed by electronic ballot at that meeting are procedurally defective and of no force and effect pending confirmation at a properly conducted meeting (paragraph 7.1).

The HOA is directed to convene a fresh general meeting within 30 days of delivery of the order, on not less than 14 days' written notice under clause 5.4, conducted by show of hands under clause 5.6, to reconsider **all** resolutions purportedly passed on 13 July 2026 — **including the determination of the number of trustees and the election of the committee (paragraph 7.2)**.

We accept, as the order expressly provides, that the trustees declared elected on 13 July remain in office until that meeting, and that nothing in the order invalidates any act of the committee taken in the interim (paragraph 7.11.1). That is a holding arrangement, not a new mandate.

## 2. The interim committee has no mandate from the past AGM

The interim arrangement is finite. It ends at the fresh general meeting, which must be held within the 30 days the order allows. The election of office-bearers must then be run afresh, with the number of trustees and the committee decided by the members under the Constitution.

Because the resolutions of 13 July are of no force pending confirmation, the mandate drawn from that meeting has expired. In substance, the effect is the same as a dissolution of that mandate: a fresh election must be held at which the nomination of trustees is considered. The committee cannot claim authority from the 13 July AGM to carry it beyond the fresh meeting, nor to pre-empt the decisions the members must make there.

It matters that the committee appears to have misunderstood the order in this respect. The adjudicator is explicit that my nomination "is to be considered at the general meeting" ordered under paragraph 7.2 (paragraph 7.5). Every person nominated at that meeting must be considered by the presiding officer, without discrimination and applying only the criteria the Constitution specifies — registered ownership of a residential erf, or spousal status (clauses 6.1 and 6.2). No "paid-up" requirement exists. A nomination may not be set aside for any other reason, and every nomination must be put to the members.

## 3. Why voting must be by show of hands

Clause 5.6 is peremptory: "At all meetings votes on any matter shall be by show of hands of those present." The adjudicator found that no show of hands is recorded on any item at the 13 July meeting (paragraph 6.8).

The method is not a formality. Show of hands makes the vote visible: everyone present can see the level of support for a resolution or for a candidate, and members who are bound by the outcome are entitled to a vote conducted in the manner they have agreed and declared in their presence (paragraph 6.10). That is what makes an election free and fair in a community of owners.

**We reject secret ballots** for these elections. Our Constitution does not authorise them, and a preference poll by email or an online platform is not a substitute for a vote by show of hands at a general meeting. Any other method would require a constitutional amendment complying with clause 12 and approved by the City of Cape Town under clause 16.3. None exists.

## 4. The HOA's own record of the ballot failure

The HOA's failure to observe the prescribed protocol is recorded in the order. In its own submissions the HOA said that members who could not access the digital platform were given paper ballots, that those ballots were "processed and captured into the voting system after the meeting" (paragraph 5.4.2), and that all paper ballots received were captured on the UniMeet platform on 15 July 2026 (paragraph 5.6.2).

The adjudicator's finding is unambiguous:

> "On the Respondent's own version, the paper ballots of members who were physically present were captured on 15 July 2026, two days after the meeting, whereas the minutes record each resolution as approved at the meeting. The results declared to the members were therefore declared before the votes of all those present had been counted." (paragraph 6.9)

The order records the same point again: paper ballots were issued to members who could not use the platform, they were captured on 15 July 2026, and the results were declared at the meeting **before they were captured** (paragraph 6.29). The HOA could not produce separate totals of electronic votes and paper ballots for each resolution, so it cannot be determined whether the outcome declared reflected the votes cast (paragraph 6.9).

## 5. Our Constitution is the basis on which we organise

Soralia is a common-law homeowners' association, not a body corporate under the Body Corporates Act. We do not organise as a community by default or by administrative convenience: we organise under the Constitution, which is the agreement members have made with one another about how decisions are taken, who may stand for election and how votes are counted.

We recognise the need to refresh the Constitution. The AGM notice of 13 July 2026 itself identified vague wording, overly broad trustee powers and rules that do not align with it. But a refresh must be done openly, by the procedure the Constitution prescribes — a special resolution under clause 12 and, where required, the approval of the City under clause 16.3 — with proper legal review and members' participation. We reject the latest attempt to circumvent that process and the process ordered by the CSOS.

## 6. What members should expect from the fresh meeting

We have read the Trustees' notice of 25 November. It says the HOA "remains fully operational", that the Board "has not been dissolved and remains in office", and that a circular about a general meeting will follow. It calls concerns about dissolution "misleading information" and asks residents to refrain from circulating it.

Nobody has said the HOA is dissolved. The HOA does remain fully operational, and the Trustees do remain in office — the order itself says so (paragraph 7.11.1). What has expired is the mandate they drew from the 13 July AGM, because the outcome of that meeting is of no force pending confirmation. The adjudicator is quite candid about the reason for allowing an interim committee: until the fresh meeting is held, the trustees declared elected on 13 July remain in office "so that the HOA is not left without a committee" (paragraph 6.14). That is a caretaker arrangement to keep the village administered. It is not a renewal of the mandate the members gave on 13 July, and it cannot be used to pre-empt the election the members must now run.

Our position is narrow and we state it plainly:

- the circular must say that the meeting is held under the order of CSOS in Case 518015 and that it will reconsider the number of trustees and the election of the committee;
- the notice and agenda must be delivered in time for the meeting to be held within the 30 days allowed, with the election as a separately stated item;
- nominations must be open to all owners and spouses on the criteria in clauses 6.1 and 6.2 — the sole eligibility criteria, with no "paid-up" requirement — and nominations from the floor must be accepted;
- voting must be by show of hands, with tellers appointed by the meeting and not by the managing agent, and proxies in the form clause 14 allows;
- no sixth trustee should be co-opted or appointed before the members have decided the number of trustees and the election at the fresh meeting; and
- any urgent financial business should be dealt with at that meeting, not at a separate meeting that pre-empts what the order requires;
- members must have a direct email address for the trustees. We cannot be required to direct complaints about the managing agent to the managing agent; and
- the managing agent's role at the meeting is administrative support, not governance.

We are also tabling the enclosed member motion on further irregularities: it asks the meeting to reaffirm clause 5.5, under which the elected chairman presides, or a chairman elected by the members present in his absence, and excludes representatives of the managing agent from chairing, ruling on eligibility or making procedural decisions at general meetings. No one should preside over their own election. A copy of the motion will be available on our website, and we ask that it be placed on the agenda of the fresh meeting.

## 7. Our standing position

We again urge supporters and members to reject automatic handovers and any breach of our constitutional dispensation and of the democratic character of our community. An account may be handed over for collection only by a minuted committee decision on that account, under clauses 8.6 and 7.6.8, after written notice, a full breakdown and a genuine opportunity to pay, agree a plan or explain hardship.

We support legitimate debt collection aimed at restoring the village's finances, and we urge members to pay their levies on time. What we oppose is the unlawful circumvention of due process and action contrary to the Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act — not legitimate collection. Those most exposed when procedure is bypassed are our seniors, persons with disabilities and military veterans, many on fixed incomes.

## 8. Not legal advice

This statement is issued by Soralia Village Community Action for discussion among members and supporters. It is not legal advice. The CSOS order itself is the authoritative text, and members should obtain their own legal advice before acting on any of it.
