Community Charter for Home Owners' Associations

Please read our proposed Community Charter. It sets out the rights of members and residents, fair financial and collection practices, and the duties of trustees and the managing agent. This is a proposal for discussion, not an adopted rule of the association.

Read the full draft Download the Charter (DOCX)

Why we drafted it

Our Constitution is founded on human dignity, equality and freedom. The Charter is our proposal for making those values practical in the day-to-day life of the village: open decisions, fair treatment, and rules that serve the community rather than the reverse.

It is explicit about money. Levies that are properly due must be paid, charges must be transparent and itemised, disputed amounts must be investigated and answered in writing, and collection must be lawful and courteous. It also makes clear that no one may be penalised for raising a concern, joining a complaint, or approaching an ombud, regulator or court.

What is in the draft

  • Founding principles — dignity, equality, accountability, openness, fairness, proportionality and community first.
  • Rights of members and residents — voice, assembly, information, participation, privacy, fair treatment in finances and debt, due process, and safety and inclusion.
  • Duties of members and residents — rights come with responsibilities, including paying levies that are properly due.
  • Obligations of trustees and directors — fiduciary duties, transparency, reasons, and never using the association's powers to retaliate against a critic.
  • Obligations of the managing agent — a written mandate, disclosed fees, two-way communication, and no charges or collection steps outside that mandate.
  • Communication standards — clear notices, a regular members' forum, fairly moderated platforms and a published complaints procedure.
  • Resolving disputes — conversation first, then an internal complaint, mediation, and the external remedies that are always open.
  • Adoption, amendment and review — adoption by members, annual reporting on compliance, and review at least every three years.

Full draft text

Draft 1 for discussion. Prepared for review by residents and a legal practitioner before adoption.

Preamble

We, the owners and residents of this community, live under a Constitution founded on human dignity, equality and freedom, and on accountable, responsive and open government. A residential community is not a private kingdom. It is a small society in which neighbours share space, cost and responsibility, and in which the exercise of power must be answerable to those it affects.

We recognise that levies, rules and management exist to serve the community, and not the reverse. We adopt this Charter to protect the dignity and voice of every person who lives here, to keep decision-making open and fair, and to make sure that the collection of money never displaces the building of community.

This Charter is guided by the spirit of ubuntu: a person is a person through other persons.

Part 1. Founding principles

  • Dignity. Every owner, resident, tenant, domestic worker, visitor and employee is entitled to respect for their inherent dignity.
  • Equality. Rules and enforcement apply equally. No person is treated less favourably on grounds such as race, gender, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, culture, language, belief, family status, or because they are a tenant or a levy-payer in arrears.
  • Accountability. Trustees, directors, committees and managing agents are accountable to the members for what they do and do not do.
  • Openness. Information belongs to the community. Secrecy is the exception and must be justified.
  • Fairness. No member is penalised, charged, restricted or sanctioned without notice, a genuine opportunity to respond, reasons, and a route of appeal.
  • Proportionality. Any sanction must be reasonable and proportionate, and no more severe than needed to achieve a legitimate purpose.
  • Community first. The purpose of the association is the safe, well-maintained, peaceful and neighbourly enjoyment of the community by its residents.

Part 2. Rights of members and residents

2.1 Voice and expression

  • Every member and resident may speak, write, publish and campaign on community matters, including criticising the trustees, the managing agent and the way the scheme is run.
  • No person may be threatened, victimised, penalised, fined, restricted in service or treated adversely because they raised a concern, asked a question, requested information, joined a complaint, or approached an ombud, regulator or court.
  • Rules may not be used to suppress lawful dissent. Dignity and civility are expected of everyone, and criticism of conduct and decisions is not a breach of them.

2.2 Assembly and association

  • Residents may meet, form committees, interest groups and social, cultural, environmental, religious or sports groups, and may petition the trustees.
  • On reasonable request and at reasonable cost, the association will allow the use of common areas for resident gatherings and will provide members with a way to contact one another (subject to the Protection of Personal Information Act, see 2.5).
  • A petition signed by a stated percentage of members (proposed: 10%) obliges the trustees to table the matter at the next meeting, or to call a special general meeting.

2.3 Information and transparency

Within a stated period (proposed: 14 days) of a written request, any member may inspect and receive copies of:

  • the constitution, rules and any amendments;
  • minutes of trustee and general meetings;
  • annual financial statements, budgets, management accounts and levy calculations;
  • the managing agent's mandate and fee agreement, and a statement of any commission, mark-up, referral fee or other benefit the agent or its affiliates receive from the community's spend;
  • insurance policies, tenders, quotations and contracts;
  • the member's own account and ledger, with supporting detail.

The association will publish routinely: agendas and minutes, quarterly financial summaries, the annual budget, contractor appointments over an agreed value, and a statement of total levies in arrears and the cost of collection.

2.4 Participation and fair governance

  • Members are entitled to meaningful notice of meetings, a published agenda, reasonable opportunity to speak, access to proxies and, where practical, participation by electronic means.
  • Trustees are elected by members in open, properly run elections. No person may be disqualified, or prevented from standing or voting, other than on grounds set out in the law or the constitution.
  • Significant decisions (major levy increases, special levies, changes to rules, long-term contracts, appointment or renewal of the managing agent) require prior notice, a stated rationale and a member vote or consultation as the law and constitution provide.

2.5 Privacy and protection of personal information

  • Personal information of members and residents (including arrears, disputes, access-control and CCTV data) is processed only as the law allows and only for legitimate community purposes.
  • Arrears lists, names of defaulters and details of disputes will not be published to the community or notice boards.
  • Access-control, biometric and CCTV systems must be disclosed, proportionate, secured and governed by a written policy available to members.

2.6 Fair treatment in finances and debt

  • Levies and charges are set by a transparent budget approved in the manner required, and each member receives a clear, itemised statement.
  • A member who disputes a charge may do so in writing. Disputed amounts will be investigated and answered in writing within a stated period (proposed: 21 days), with supporting calculations.
  • Before any collection steps, the member is given written notice, the full breakdown of the debt, and a reasonable opportunity to pay, to agree a payment plan or to explain hardship.
  • Collection will be carried out lawfully, courteously and without harassment, humiliation, threats, or disclosure to third parties. Interest and collection costs are limited to what the law and the constitution allow and are not imposed as a source of revenue for the association or its agents.
  • Access to a person's home, to essential services and to the ordinary use of common property (including access for family, carers and emergency services) is not withheld as a collection tool except as the law expressly permits.
  • The association will adopt a written hardship policy. The pursuit of members in financial difficulty is a last resort, preceded by engagement.

2.7 Just administrative process and discipline

Before any fine, penalty, restriction or other adverse action:

  • written notice of the alleged breach, the rule relied on and the evidence;
  • a reasonable period to respond;
  • an opportunity to be heard, in person or in writing, by a decision-maker who is not conflicted;
  • written reasons;
  • a right of internal appeal and access to external resolution.

Fines and penalties must be proportionate, rule-based and applied consistently, with a record kept and reported to members.

2.8 Safety, access and inclusion

  • The community will be run so that persons with disabilities, children, older residents and vulnerable people can live there with dignity and with reasonable accommodation.
  • Security services, access control and patrols must respect the dignity of residents, visitors, domestic workers, contractors and delivery persons. Searching, profiling or humiliating people is not permitted.
  • Domestic workers and service providers are entitled to dignified treatment, and to access that is not arbitrarily restricted.

Part 3. Duties of members and residents

Rights come with responsibilities. Every member and resident undertakes to:

  • pay levies and charges that are properly due, and raise any dispute in good time;
  • respect the rights, privacy, safety and quiet enjoyment of neighbours;
  • comply with the rules of the association as lawfully made;
  • take part in the life of the community as far as they are able, including attending meetings and voting;
  • raise concerns first through the channels provided where this is reasonable, and approach external bodies in good faith;
  • treat trustees, managing agents, staff and each other courteously, and without abuse or intimidation;
  • take care of the common property and the environment.

Part 4. Obligations of trustees and directors

Trustees and directors owe fiduciary duties to the association and its members, and will:

  • act honestly, in good faith and in the best interests of the community as a whole;
  • exercise care, skill and diligence, and avoid conflicts of interest;
  • declare any personal, family or business interest in a matter, and recuse themselves from decisions in which they are interested;
  • keep proper records, supervise the managing agent, and be able to explain every material decision to members;
  • consult residents before significant decisions and give reasons afterwards;
  • never use the association's powers to retaliate against a critic.

Part 5. Obligations of the managing agent

A managing agent is the agent of the association, appointed by and answerable to it, and not the ruler of the community. The agent will:

  • act within a written mandate that is available to all members, and that is reviewed and re-tendered at defined intervals (proposed: no longer than three years);
  • treat every resident with courtesy and respond to written queries within a stated period (proposed: 5 working days for acknowledgement, 15 working days for substantive reply);
  • keep communication two-way, including a named contact person, an escalation route, and regular open resident forums;
  • disclose all fees, commissions, rebates and relationships between the agent and any supplier, including related parties;
  • hold community funds in properly designated accounts and give trustees real-time or monthly access to them;
  • not collect debts or impose charges outside the mandate, and not apply fines, restrictions or legal steps without the authority of the trustees;
  • comply with applicable regulation, including the laws governing property practitioners, debt collection, consumer protection and personal information;
  • co-operate fully with ombud, regulator and court processes and with any lawful request from members.

The association will not renew, and may terminate, an agent who is repeatedly in breach of this Charter, subject to the law and the contract.

Part 6. Communication standards

  • Communication is two-way. Residents can ask questions and receive answers, and not only receive notices.
  • Notices will be clear, plain-language and timely, and will go out through multiple channels (email, noticeboard, and where appropriate a community app or messaging group), with provision for residents without digital access.
  • A members' forum is held at least quarterly, to which trustees and the agent come to answer questions.
  • Any community communication platform will be moderated fairly and openly. It will not be used to silence criticism, and moderators will not delete lawful dissent.
  • A simple, published complaints procedure will give every complaint a reference number, an owner, a deadline and a written outcome.

Part 7. Resolving disputes

  • Conversation first. Most disputes can be resolved by talking.
  • Internal complaint to the trustees, answered in writing within a stated period.
  • Mediation or conciliation by an independent person, at shared or association cost.
  • External remedies are always open: the Community Schemes Ombud Service, the Debt Collectors Council, the Information Regulator, the Property Practitioners Regulatory Authority, and the courts. Using them is never a ground for retaliation.
  • No rule, mandate or contract may ban or penalise a member for approaching an external body.

Part 8. Adoption, amendment and review

  • This Charter is adopted by resolution of the members at a general meeting and incorporated into, or referenced by, the association's rules to the extent the law permits.
  • It is available to every member and every new owner and tenant, and is read together with the association's constitution, the rules and the law. If there is any conflict, the Constitution and the law prevail.
  • The trustees will report annually to members on compliance with this Charter, including complaints received and resolved, fines imposed, arrears and collection costs, information requests and response times.
  • The Charter is reviewed at least every three years, with member consultation.

Drafting notes (not part of the Charter)

  • Legal status. A charter adopted by resolution does not automatically bind a managing agent or override the scheme's constitution. To give it teeth it should be (a) adopted as, or incorporated into, the conduct rules or constitution according to the amendment procedure that applies, and (b) referenced in the managing agent's mandate and any renewal.
  • Which regime applies. The right route differs between a sectional title scheme (body corporate), a homeowners' association (often a non-profit company) and a share block or retirement scheme. Check which statute and rules govern your scheme.
  • Key South African sources to anchor the text: Constitution ss 7-10, 14, 16-18, 32-34 (dignity, equality, privacy, expression, assembly, association, information, just administrative action, access to courts) and s 8(2) on horizontal application; Community Schemes Ombud Service Act 9 of 2011; Sectional Titles Schemes Management Act 8 of 2011 and its Prescribed Management and Conduct Rules; Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013; Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008; Property Practitioners Act 22 of 2019; Debt Collectors Act 114 of 1998; Companies Act 71 of 2008 (directors' duties, if the HOA is a company).
  • Percentages and time limits in brackets are suggestions for the community to settle.
  • Have an attorney experienced in community schemes review the final text before adoption.
