Our statement on the interim committee and the fresh election explains what the order means for members, and why voting must be by show of hands.

Case 518015 concerned the annual general meeting held on 13 July 2026. On 13 November 2026 the Community Schemes Ombud Service issued an order in which the central relief I sought was granted. Prayers 1 to 5 were granted; prayers 6 to 9 were refused. There is no order as to costs.

What the order grants

  • The electronic vote was not authorised. Clause 5.6 of the Constitution prescribes voting by show of hands. The use of an electronic voting system at the 13 July 2026 meeting was not authorised by that clause, and all resolutions passed by electronic ballot are procedurally defective and of no force and effect pending confirmation at a properly conducted meeting.
  • A fresh general meeting must be held. The HOA is directed to convene one within 30 days of delivery of the order, on at least 14 days' written notice as clause 5.4 requires. It must be conducted by show of hands under clause 5.6, unless an alternative method has been properly authorised by a constitutional amendment complying with clause 12 and approved by the City of Cape Town under clause 16.3. The meeting must reconsider all resolutions purportedly passed on 13 July 2026, including the number of trustees and the election of the committee.
  • Voting rights are protected. The automatic suspension of my voting rights at the 13 July meeting, on the basis of a disputed arrear balance that was before the CSOS in Case 518005, was unlawful. No member's voting rights may be suspended under clause 5.9 on the basis of a balance that includes charges under dispute before the CSOS. For that purpose, the restriction is measured against levies in arrears for 30 days or more; charges that are not levies and are under dispute before the CSOS are excluded.
  • Eligibility for election is declared. Clauses 6.1 and 6.2 contain the sole eligibility criteria for committee membership — registered ownership of a residential erf, or spousal status. No provision of the Constitution disqualifies a member from standing for election on grounds of levy arrears. My disqualification at the 13 July meeting was unlawful and without constitutional foundation.
  • My nomination goes to the fresh meeting. I am to be treated as having been eligible for election on 13 July 2026, and my nomination is to be considered at the general meeting the HOA has been ordered to convene.

What was refused

The adjudicator refused relief that I be permitted to serve on a sub-committee while the levy dispute was unresolved; relief for written reasons for the dissolution of the Water Committee and its reinstatement; relief directing Trafalgar Property Management (Pty) Ltd to give all members a written account of the voting process; and further or alternative relief. Trafalgar was not cited as a party to the complaint.

What happens in the meantime

Pending the fresh general meeting, the trustees declared elected on 13 July 2026 remain in office, and nothing in the order invalidates any act of the committee taken in the interim. The increased levies that took effect on 1 August 2026 are not to be reversed — but if the fresh meeting does not confirm the increase, the HOA must credit each member's account with the increase charged, within 30 days after that meeting.

The order takes effect on the date it is delivered to the parties. Section 57 of the CSOS Act allows an appeal to the High Court on a question of law, to be lodged within 30 days of delivery.

What this order does not decide

It does not revisit the order in Case 518005, which directed the HOA to reverse disputed receipting and arrears-review fees on my account and to recalculate the interest on them. It does not decide whether automatic handovers are lawful in general. Our position remains that treating an account as handed over by calendar date or blanket resolution is contrary to clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 of the Constitution. We support legitimate debt collection and urge members to pay levies that are properly due on time; we oppose only the unlawful circumvention of due process and action contrary to the Constitution and the Debt Collectors Act.
