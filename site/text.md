Soralia Village is our home. We protect each other.
Our position is clear: automatic handovers are ultra vires clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 of the Soralia Village Constitution. Clause 8.6 permits the committee to hand over a debt only if it has been outstanding for more than 30 days, and only by an active committee decision. Clause 7.6.8 requires committee approval for each routine recovery action. A blanket resolution or calendar trigger cannot replace those individual decisions.
We all feel the pressure of the large municipal bill. Automatic handovers of arrears accounts may look like a quick fix, but they do not solve the real problem. They simply move the stress onto individual households — especially the most vulnerable among us.
Pensioners on fixed incomes, residents living with disabilities, and military veterans who have already given so much should not be the first to face collection fees, demand letters and credit pressure. Our history and our Constitution remind us that dignity and fairness matter. A healthy community does not squeeze its most vulnerable members while the bigger municipal debt remains untouched.
There is a better way.
We call on our Board to:

Recognise that automatic handovers are ultra vires clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 of our Constitution
Stop automatic handovers for pensioners, disabled persons and military veterans
Introduce proper social assessment of accounts before any collection steps
Focus collective energy on engaging the City of Cape Town about the municipal bill itself — bulk meters, leaks, possible relief and long-term solutions
