Soralia Village Governance & Transition

Managing Agent Shift, Board Dynamics, and Institutional Recovery

Draft resolution for members

Reaffirming constitutional presiding authority (Clause 5.5)

The article below sets out our analysis and a phase-by-phase recovery roadmap. A companion draft member motion would reaffirm that only the elected Chairperson — or a homeowner elected by the meeting — may chair general meetings, and confine the managing agent to administrative support. It is a draft for a proposer and seconder to complete, not an adopted resolution.

Soralia Village Homeowners Association stands at a critical juncture in its institutional life. Over the past eighteen months, the estate has experienced a series of structural shocks: the termination of its long-standing managing agency contract, consecutive trustee resignations amidst financial controversies, an unprecedented operating deficit, and escalating legal disputes before the Community Schemes Ombud Service. These intersecting crises reflect deeper systemic vulnerabilities in common-law homeowners' associations — specifically, the reliance on remote property management, the erosion of institutional memory, the over-extension of trustee authority, and a breakdown in member communication.

Navigating this transition requires an objective examination of recent history, an evaluation of administrative overreach, and a clear, forward-looking governance framework drawn from best practices across South African residential schemes.

1. The Executive & Administrative Transition: From Residentia to Trafalgar

For years, administrative support for Soralia Village was provided by Residentia Trust (Pty) Ltd under portfolio manager Trudie Oosthuizen. However, operational friction and cost concerns began to surface during 2025. While Residentia provided established billing systems and administrative routines, estate records indicate growing member scrutiny regarding the cost-effectiveness of their contract, which stood at R23,286 per month (including CSOS submissions, payroll, and WeConnectU licensing, but excluding stationery and arrears notifications).

In late 2025, Residentia Trust served formal notice terminating its contract with the Association effective 30 November 2025. This forced the Board of Trustees to initiate an urgent recruitment and benchmarking process for new management.

The Appointment of Trafalgar Property Management

Following Residentia's exit, Trafalgar Property Management Proprietary Limited was appointed as the HOA's managing agent, operating from its regional offices at M5 Park in Maitland. Under the new management agreement, Trafalgar’s annual management fee was established at R265,599 for 2025 (budgeted at R266,230 for 2026, reflecting a scheduled 6% annual increase).

While Trafalgar brought corporate scale and specialized scheme management infrastructure, the transition introduced unexpected operational challenges:

The Remote Management Gap: Unlike an onsite estate manager, Trafalgar functions strictly as an offsite administrative processor. Day-to-day oversight of estate staff, gate infrastructure, and maintenance contractors remained placed squarely on volunteer trustees, creating an operational bottleneck.

Dual Capacity Conflicts in Arrears Recovery: Trafalgar immediately sought to activate its debt collection capacity under the Debt Collectors Act 114 of 1998. On 15 July 2026, the Board executed a blanket "Hand Over Resolution" delegating "complete discretion" to Trafalgar to hand over accounts overdue by 90 days and apply Annexure B collection fees and receipting charges directly to member statements. This shift from Residentia's softer recovery approach to automated, fee-generating collection created immediate friction with homeowners.

Procedural Boundary Blurring: Rather than restricting its role to administrative support under trustee direction, Trafalgar representatives took a leading role in general meeting proceedings, culminating in managing agent representatives presiding over the July 2026 Annual General Meeting.

2. Board Instability & Institutional Memory Erosion: A History of Resignations and Scandals

The administrative changeover occurred against a backdrop of severe board turnover. Between July 2025 and mid-2026, the Board of Trustees experienced consecutive resignations that dismantled portfolio continuity and severely weakened the estate's institutional memory.

The Petty Cash Controversy and Financial Accountability

The suspension and resignation of Trustee Yumna Daniels in August 2025 exposed critical weaknesses in the estate's internal financial controls. The controversy centered on R15,835.55 in un-reconciled purchases charged to the HOA bank card during June and July 2025. While meeting records explicitly note that Ms. Daniels was recognized as a dedicated trustee and was not formally accused of criminal intent, the prolonged absence of supporting receipts forced the Board to suspend her to preserve fiscal integrity.

This incident highlighted a structural vulnerability: trustees making operational purchases out of personal funds or unmonitored cards due to the absence of clear expenditure thresholds and formal petty cash management protocols.

We note that her differing statement exists so that members have the full picture; those allegations belong in the forum in which they can properly be tested.

The Exit of Leadership and Loss of Context

Shortly thereafter, in September 2025, long-serving Chairperson Lawrence Manasse announced his resignation and departure from Soralia Village after selling his home. Mr. Manasse had been the primary driving force behind major capital infrastructure initiatives, including the estate's automated security gate installation. His departure created an immediate information void; subsequent Board minutes record that the incoming Board struggled to address owner queries regarding gate contracts and supplier engagements because project documentation had not been formally archived in a centralized repository.

Most recently, the resignation of Gardening Trustee Cheryl-Anne Gordon marked the loss of another experienced board member. During her tenure, Ms. Gordon established vital operational controls: securing compliant employment contracts and leave tracking for ground staff, conducting formal equipment stocktakes, and introducing a photographic water meter reading system in January 2026 that eliminated historical billing discrepancies and enabled rapid leak detection. While her decision to step down and maintain private reasons must be fully respected, her exit underscores the unsustainable burden placed on individual volunteer trustees who manage complex estate portfolios without professional onsite support.

3. The Governance Strain: Overreaching Administration, Dissolved Committees, and CSOS Interventions

Following the election of the 2025/2026 Board and Janine Koopman’s assumption of the Chair, governance challenges escalated into formal legal disputes before the Community Schemes Ombud Service (CSOS).

The Dissolution of the Water Committee

A central point of contention among homeowners was the unilateral dissolution of the estate's informal Water Committee. Established to investigate the estate’s single largest financial drain —municipal water and effluent charges totaling R1,999,688 against a R285,636 operating deficit in 2025 — the working group was exploring dual reticulation, bulk meter recalibration, and individual City metering.

Despite the Chairperson’s own Handover Report identifying bulk water investigations as an "outstanding matter requiring urgent attention," the working group was suppressed without a formal minuted board resolution or member consultation. This action fueled perceptions of autocratic governance and stalled critical infrastructure solutions.

The July 2026 AGM Irregularities

The governance strain culminated at the Annual General Meeting on 13 July 2026, leading to formal CSOS filings (Case 518005 and Case 518015) lodged by homeowner David Robert Lewis:

Managing Agent Presiding Over General Meeting: In direct conflict with Clause 5.5 of the Constitution — which explicitly dictates that meetings must be chaired by the elected Trustee Chairperson or a member elected by the floor — representatives from Trafalgar assumed the chair and issued binding procedural rulings.

Disqualification of Nominees and Disenfranchisement: The meeting leadership disqualified registered owners from standing for trustee election based on "eligibility declarations" not found in the Constitution, while simultaneously suspending the voting rights of members under Clause 5.9 using balances inflated by disputed debt-collection fees.

Voting Procedure Deficiencies: Electronic voting systems were utilized without formal constitutional authorization under Clause 12 (which requires a 75% special resolution and municipal approval for rule changes), and paper ballots were captured post-meeting rather than reconciled openly on the floor.

4. Lessons from Model Schemes: Institutional Pillars for Sustainable Recovery

To overcome these repeating cycles of friction, Soralia Village must draw on the proven governance structures of successful residential communities and common-law HOAs across South Africa. Healthy schemes maintain a clear division between legislative oversight (the Board), professional operations (the Estate Manager), and administrative processing (the Managing Agent).

The Recommended Tri-Partite Governance Model

The Board of Trustees (Policy & Oversight): Responsible for strategic vision, policy decisions, member approvals, and exercising ultimate fiduciary duty under Clause 7.1 of the Constitution. Fiduciary discretion — such as approving legal handovers or deciding to sue—cannot be blanket-delegated to a commercial managing agent that profits from collection fees.

The Estate Manager (Onsite Operations): Responsible for staff supervision, vendor oversight, daily maintenance, and immediate repairs. Model estates recognize that volunteer homeowners cannot act as full-time site managers. Appointing a dedicated, onsite Estate Manager provides human supervision over security, landscaping, and contractors, ensuring accountability without burning out volunteer trustees.

The Managing Agent (Admin & Financial Processing): Responsible for levy invoicing, monthly financial statements, statutory compliance filings, and trust accounting.

Constitutional Supremacy: Conduct rules and administrative resolutions are legally subordinate to the certified Constitution. Rules created by boards or managing agents that conflict with constitutional provisions are ultra vires and void ab initio.

Proactive Utility Management: Leading schemes eliminate bulk municipal exposure by aggressively transitioning to individual household metering, photographic leak detection, and non-potable dual reticulation for irrigation and sanitation.

5. Actionable Roadmap: Restoring Transparency, Financial Balance, and Community Trust

Rebuilding Soralia Village requires a structured, phase-by-phase recovery strategy that addresses immediate legal non-compliance while constructing a sustainable operational foundation.

Phase 1: Immediate Governance & Legal Alignment (Months 1–3)

Reaffirm Constitutional Meeting Authority: Formally enforce Clause 5.5 at all future general meetings. Restrict Trafalgar and any managing agent strictly to secretarial and administrative support, ensuring only the elected Chairperson or an elected homeowner presides.

Rescind Ultra Vires Resolutions: Repeal the 15 July 2026 blanket Hand Over Resolution. Replace it with a constitutionally compliant process under Clause 7.6.8 and Clause 8.6, requiring minuted, case-by-case trustee review before any debt is handed over for legal collection.

Re-Convene Special Interest Sub-Committees: Re-establish the Water Committee and Architectural Advisory Working Groups as recognized sub-committees under Clause 7.6.7, encouraging voluntary member expertise to solve pressing estate challenges.

Phase 2: Operational & Financial Restructuring (Months 3–6)

Conduct an Onsite Estate Manager Feasibility Study: Evaluate funding models for an onsite Estate Manager. Offsetting this role through operational efficiency, contractor oversight savings, and eliminated vendor travel surcharges will directly improve estate upkeep and staff productivity.

Execute Municipal Utility Reduction Interventions:

Mandate the continuation of photographic water meter logging established by the gardening portfolio.

Formalize engagement with the City of Cape Town to transition from bulk metering to direct individual household metering.

Finalize technical specifications for dual reticulation (using non-potable groundwater for common area irrigation and toilet flushing).

Implement Strict Financial Control Thresholds: Adopt a binding resolution limiting unbudgeted trustee expenditure to a strict threshold (e.g., R20,000 emergency limit per Clause 12 restrictions), supported by mandatory dual-signoff on all HOA bank accounts.

Phase 3: Long-Term Institutional Modernization (Months 6–12)

Comprehensive Constitutional Overhaul: Execute a full constitutional review under Clause 12 (requiring a 75% member special resolution and City of Cape Town approval) to remove vague trustee powers, update fine schedules, establish clear conflict-of-interest disclosures, and formally authorize modern digital voting procedures.

Deploy a Centralized Digital Community Hub: Replace chaotic WhatsApp groups and scattered emails with a unified, transparent Community Informatics Hub (such as the proposed Netbones platform). A centralized portal preserves institutional memory, digitizes maintenance ticketing, stores asset registers, and provides transparent financial reporting to every homeowner.

Conclusion

The challenges confronting Soralia Village are not insurmountable; they are the predictable result of rapid administrative transition, fragmented leadership, and outdated governance mechanisms. By realigning administrative practices with the Constitution, empowering voluntary member participation, and establishing professional onsite management, the Association can restore fiscal stability and build an inclusive, resilient community for all residents.
