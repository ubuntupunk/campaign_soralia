STATEMENT FOR SORALIA VILLAGE MEMBERS
02 November 2026

CSOS orders reversal of disputed collection fees; member reserves position on automatic handovers

I write as an ordinary homeowner and member of Soralia Village HOA, not as a representative of any body.

On 2 November 2026 (today) the Community Schemes Ombud Service (CSOS) issued an adjudication order in Case 518005. It directed the HOA to reverse the “receipting fees” charged to my account from May to August 2026, and the “arrears account review” charges from June to August 2026. It also directed the HOA to recalculate the interest charged on those amounts. The adjudicator found that these charges had not been substantiated under the applicable debt collectors’ tariff, and that resolutions adopted on 15 July 2026 could not retrospectively authorise earlier charges.

The adjudicator declined to rule on Trafalgar’s practices across other schemes, because that falls outside what CSOS can grant. The order does not decide whether automatic handovers are lawful in general.

My position is this. Treating an account as handed over for collection by calendar date, or under a blanket resolution, is contrary to our own Constitution. Clause 8.6 says the committee “may” hand over a debt outstanding for more than 30 days. Clause 7.6.8 requires committee approval for routine recovery action. Each handover should be a committee decision on an individual account. The 15 July 2026 Hand Over Resolution is ultra vires the Constitution because it conflicts with clause 7.6.8 (which requires committee approval for each routine recovery action and cannot be delegated in blanket terms) and clause 8.6 (which requires a discretionary committee decision that a debt outstanding for more than 30 days be handed over), and because it impermissibly delegates trustee fiduciary duties to a conflicted third party.

I am particularly concerned about seniors, people with disabilities, and military veterans, many of whom live on fixed incomes and may pay late or in parts through no fault of their own. They are the members most exposed to automatic collection fees. We must not turn our HOA into a pure debt-collection machine. Let's tackle the sizeable MUNI bill together.

I reserve all my options, including further complaints to the CSOS and the Council for Debt Collectors (CDC). I invite the trustees to review the 15 July handover resolution against the Constitution.

Read [why I approached the CSOS](why.html) for the background to this complaint.

Members should check their own statements for similar charges and query anything unclear in writing. This statement is not legal advice, and each account stands on its own facts.
