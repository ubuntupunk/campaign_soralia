The story

Soralia Village is a community of homes in Capricorn, Cape Town. Many of us are seniors, people with disabilities and military veterans living on fixed incomes. Some pay late or in parts through no fault of their own: a pension arrives on a certain day, a grant is delayed, a medical bill lands the same month as the levy.

Until recently, a member in that position could expect a human conversation with the Board. Since our managing agents changed, that has stopped. Accounts are now handed over for collection automatically, by calendar date and under a blanket resolution, with no committee decision about the person or the account. Each handover adds collection charges and interest to what a household already owes.

What we have already tested

We have not been guessing. Charges of exactly this kind — “receipting fees” and “arrears account review” — have already been put to the Community Schemes Ombud Service, and the outcome was that they could not be substantiated under the applicable debt collectors’ tariff.

We are not able to set out the details of that matter on this page while it is still live. What we can say is that the same charges appear on statement after statement in this Village, and the same answer should apply to them.

The wider question — whether an account may be handed over automatically, by calendar date, under a blanket resolution, with no decision about the person — has not been decided by anyone. That is the question this campaign exists to force. Our position is that it conflicts with our own Constitution: clause 8.6 says the committee “may” hand over a debt, and clause 7.6.8 requires committee approval for each routine recovery action. A blanket resolution is not a decision about a person.

Why we need your help

Taking on a managing agent and a collections machine is hard to do alone, and those most affected are least able to. One homeowner cannot carry it for everyone. The Village needs a proper support organisation with a bank account: a structure that can help members understand what they are being charged, and stand beside those who can’t manage it themselves.

Your donation does two things

1. Forming a Village Support Organisation (VSO)

This is the long-term goal. It could become an HOA sub-project once the legality of the voting process and the Board’s decisions is determined. Funds go towards:

- Setting up properly: registration, a basic constitution and a bank account with transparent reporting
- Helping members put their own questions to the HOA in writing, in their own name
- Guiding members through the free external complaint processes — CSOS and the Council for Debt Collectors — in their own name
- Legal opinion on the Constitution and the 15 July 2026 resolution
- Notices, meetings and printed material for members offline
- Engaging the City of Cape Town on the underlying municipal bill, including bulk meters, leaks and possible relief

2. Emergency grants for households in hardship

Where a household is in genuine hardship — a pension that did not stretch, a medical bill that landed in the same month as the levy — part of the funds will be set aside for a once-off emergency grant. Assessed case by case, with priority for seniors, people with disabilities and veterans. Reported openly in total, with no names.

Proposed split: up to 65% for organisation set-up and member support, the balance for the emergency grant fund.

What success looks like

- Handovers decided account by account, as clauses 7.6.8 and 8.6 require
- No automatic handovers for pensioners, disabled persons and veterans
- Social assessment before any collection step
- Unsubstantiated charges reversed
- A lasting support organisation for the next member in trouble

This campaign is run by Soralia Village Community Action, not on behalf of the HOA or any official body. Nothing here is legal advice, and each account stands on its own facts. Members who want the underlying documents can ask us directly and we will send them.

We believe in dignity, not displacement. Thank you for standing with our Village.
